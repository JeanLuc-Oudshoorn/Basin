# Basin 220M: hybrid backbone, direct 112-step head and a new training recipe

Date: 2026-11-15. Implements the training and architecture items of the
[efficiency review](EFFICIENCY_REVIEW_2026-11-11.md) (all except B4), a 220M model
size, a 7-token (112-timestep) forecast with equal weight per timestep, and the
conclusions drawn from the HART paper (`docs/HART_hybrid_rkwv_transformer_tsfm.pdf`).

Status: implemented, unit-tested on CPU, and run end to end on the GPU on a small
corpus. **Not yet trained at scale**, so no accuracy claim is made here. The pilot
runs in section 7 are a few minutes each and only show that the pieces train.

The 500M model is archived in `results/500m_model/` and still loads and evaluates
with `configs/legacy_500m.json`: every new option defaults to the old behaviour.

## 1. What the fresh 500M holdout says (review item A1)

From `results/500m_model/holdout_test/test_predictions.parquet` (87,312 requests,
weights of step 848,905). Scripts: `experiments/claude_review/holdout_repeat_check.py`
and `holdout_full_context_check.py`.

**The model no longer repeats its first patch.** Distance between a model's own
patch *k* and its patch 1, in MASE units, median over requests:

| Series | patch 2 vs 1 | patch 3 vs 1 | patch 4 vs 1 | corr(patch 1, patch 2) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Basin, benchmarked 2026-11-09 (old weights) | 0.003 | 0.003 | 0.003 | 1.000 |
| Basin, current weights | 1.431 | 1.672 | 1.447 | 0.09 |
| Chronos-2 | 1.344 | 1.552 | 1.175 | −0.02 |
| Timestral | 1.246 | 1.440 | 1.157 | 0.01 |

Patches 2–4 are closer to their own actuals than a copy of patch 1 would be on
72–77% of requests, more often than for either competitor. The repetition was a
symptom of the BF16 update defect (the lead offsets could not move), not of the
head's design. The design is still weak, see section 3.

**Where Basin still trails.** Median per-request MASE, Basin relative to Timestral:

| Context points | patch 1 | patch 2 | patch 3 | patch 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ≤ 1024 (71,260 requests) | −3% | −6% | −6% | −5% |
| 1025–1984 (14,939 requests) | +5% | +6% | +8% | +12% |

Basin is ahead with short and medium history and behind with long history, and
that gap widens with the lead. Later leads carried 16%, 3% and 1% of the loss.
This is the evidence for equal lead weights and for the review's conditional item
C5 (temporal attention "only if the long-history gap is still there").

## 2. RWKV-7 initialisation

Upstream RWKV-7 has two layers of initialisation:

1. The block constructors (`RWKV_Tmix_x070.__init__`, `RWKV_CMix_x070.__init__`)
   set the decay, gate and mixing-ratio parameters and draw the projections from a
   small uniform distribution. This is what upstream documents for using the
   blocks inside another model. **Basin used exactly this**, correctly.
2. Upstream's own training entry point then calls `generate_init_weight`
   (vendored `model.py`, line 966), which overwrites part of it:

| Parameter | Constructor (500M run) | Upstream training recipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `att.receptance`, `att.value`, `ffn.key` | uniform ±0.5/√width | orthogonal, gain 1 (3.5× larger) |
| `att.key` | uniform ±0.05/√width | orthogonal, gain 0.1 (3.5× larger) |
| `att.output`, `ffn.value` | zero | zero |
| `att.ln_x` gain | 1 in every layer | ((layer + 1) / layers)^0.7 |
| decay `w0`, gates, mixing ratios | constructor values | unchanged |

So nothing was missing or broken, but the 500M run did not use the initialisation
that upstream's released models are trained from. `model.rwkv_init = "upstream"`
applies it (`apply_upstream_rwkv_init` in `train.py`); the new default uses it.
The language-model-specific parts (token embedding, vocabulary head) do not apply
to Basin. Whether it helps Basin is not established; pilot C in section 7 is the
only measurement.

## 3. Multi-token forecasting and the HART paper

**How the 500M model forecasts four patches.** One hidden vector per position; for
leads 2–4 a learned offset (3 × 1,024 = 3,072 parameters) is added before the final
LayerNorm and the same single-patch projection is reused. LayerNorm rescales its
input, so a small offset is easy to wash out, and the offsets only received
16/3/1% of the gradient.

**What HART shows.** HART forecasts by appending placeholder tokens for the future
and running them through the backbone. For RWKV-7 the recurrent state keeps being
multiplied by its transition matrix across those placeholders while little new
information is written, so history fades ("placeholder idling decay"). Alternating
attention and RWKV blocks fixes it, because attention lets the forecast positions
read the history tokens directly. The size of the effect depends on the horizon:

| Horizon group (HART table 2, MASE) | Pure RWKV | Hybrid | Gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ~30 steps (GIFT-Eval and TIME short) | 0.689 / 0.620 | 0.689 / 0.618 | none |
| ~122 steps (TIME medium) | 0.727 | 0.716 | 1.5% |
| ~480–900 steps (GIFT medium / long) | 0.749 / 0.770 | 0.727 / 0.752 | 2–3% |

**What that means for Basin at 112 steps.**

- Basin does not use placeholders, so it has no idling decay to fix. Adopting
  HART's placeholder decoding would introduce the problem and give up Basin's
  dense supervision (every position of every table is a training example).
- The transferable finding is the mechanism: a forecast should have **direct
  attention access to history** in addition to the recurrent state. At ~120 steps
  HART measures a gain of about 1.5%; at ~30 steps none. Expect a small effect.
- It lines up with Basin's own remaining weakness (long history, later leads).

**What was implemented.**

1. *Direct head* (`model.head = "direct"`, review item B2): one projection emits
   all 7 patches × 16 steps × 9 quantiles. Each lead has its own output rows; no
   offset, nothing for LayerNorm to wash out. Every lead is trained at every
   position with equal weight per timestep. One vector emitting the whole horizon
   is established practice (Chronos-Bolt and TimesFM emit 64 and 128 steps
   from one output vector).
2. *Hybrid backbone* (`model.temporal_every = 4`, review item C5): after every
   fourth RWKV block, a causal attention block over the patches of each variable,
   following HART's attention block (RMSNorm, rotary positions, sigmoid-gated
   output). Its output projection starts at zero, so a new model starts as the
   pure RWKV stack. Differences from HART: one attention block per four RWKV
   blocks instead of one per block, and no feed-forward network of its own. The
   measured cost is 0–4% of update time and 17.7M parameters.

## 4. The model

```text
history [target + covariates, up to 2,048 points]
  -> causal standardisation per variable, then asinh            (C2)
  -> 16-point patches -> patch embedding, float32               (C4)
  -> 24 RWKV-7 blocks, width 768, BF16                          (C1)
       after blocks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24:
         causal temporal attention over patches                 (C5 / HART)
         attention across variables, skipped for one variable   (C3)
       both start closed (zero-initialised output)
  -> final LayerNorm + head, float32                            (C4)
  -> 7 patches x 16 steps x 9 quantiles, sinh back to units     (B2)
```

| Part | Parameters | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 24 RWKV-7 blocks | 180,837,888 | 81.6% |
| 6 temporal attention blocks | 17,699,328 | 8.0% |
| 6 cross-variable attention blocks | 14,183,424 | 6.4% |
| Forecast head (112 steps × 9 quantiles) | 6,235,104 | 2.8% |
| Patch embedding, roles, final norm | 2,552,064 | 1.2% |
| **Total (measured)** | **221,507,808** | |

`configs/config.json`, `model` section:

| Key | Value | Review item |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `width`, `layers`, `variate_heads` | 768, 24, 12 | C1 (220M instead of 120M) |
| `output_patches`, `horizon_loss_weights` | 7, `"equal"` | 112 steps, equal weight per timestep |
| `head` | `"direct"` | B2 |
| `variate_every`, `gated_mixing` | 4, true | C3 |
| `temporal_every` | 4 | C5 and HART |
| `value_transform` | `"asinh"` | C2 |
| `fp32_io` | true | C4 |
| `rwkv_init` | `"upstream"` | section 2 |

Why 768 × 24: it is the shape of Timestral-209M (24 layers, width 768, variable
mixing every fourth block). A wider, shallower model of the same size is faster
on this GPU because small batches are dominated by per-layer overhead; see the
measurements in section 7 before changing it.

## 5. The training recipe

`configs/config.json`, `training` section:

| Key | Value | What it does | Review item |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `optimizer.parameter_groups` | `"rwkv7"` | Weight decay only on projection matrices; none on norm gains, biases, embeddings, RWKV gates and mixing ratios; `att.w0` gets 2× learning rate | B1 |
| `scheduler` | warm-up 5,000 updates, cosine to 1e-5 | Fixed schedule instead of halving on a noisy plateau | B3 |
| `epochs` | 3 | The budget the cosine schedule spans; **decide before starting** | B3 |
| `weight_average` | decay 0.9999, every 4 updates | Averaged weights are validated, selected and exported | B3 |
| `early_stopping.monitor` | `"table_mase_profile"` | One score from the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile of table MASE (below) | A2 |
| `early_stopping.patience_checks` | 12 | 12 strikes, 48 h without improvement; a safeguard, not the schedule | A2 |
| `checkpoint_token_threshold` | 3,072 | No layer is recomputed inside a microbatch (fits: peak 9.1 GiB) | C1 |

Notes:

- **The schedule cannot change on resume.** A warm-up/cosine run stores its total
  update count; `epochs` therefore has to be chosen up front. Three epochs of the
  5.3M-table corpus is an estimate of roughly 100 active hours (section 7).
- A fixed-budget run now validates its final weights when the budget ends, so the
  lowest-learning-rate weights compete for selection.
- **What counts as a strike.** Each validation table gets one MASE (the mean
  over its origins with a defined scale). The check reports the 10th percentile,
  the median and the 90th percentile across tables (`table_mase_p10`,
  `median_table_mase`, `table_mase_p90`) and their geometric mean,
  `table_mase_profile`. The geometric mean falls exactly when the three improve
  on average in relative terms, so a check that does not lower it is one strike.
  A plain average would follow the 90th percentile, which is several times
  larger than the other two. A table with a near-zero scale lands in the top
  tail and cannot move any of the three.
- A strike counts towards early stopping. It does not lower the learning rate,
  because the warm-up/cosine schedule is fixed in advance. With
  `scheduler.name = "reduce_on_plateau"` the same strikes halve the learning
  rate as in the 500M run; that needs `epochs: null` and gives up the schedule.
- MASE scores the median forecast only. The quantile forecasts are still
  reported through `trimmed_table_loss`, `median_table_loss`, `table_loss_p90`
  and `table_loss_p99`, next to the old means.
- The loss is now measured in `asinh` space. Its values are **not comparable**
  with the 500M run's validation loss.
- Learning rate 1e-4 and batch size 8 are unchanged from the 500M run and are the
  first things worth sweeping. They were not tested here.

## 6. Running it

No training data is on disk (`data/01_generated` and `data/02_processed/model`
are empty), and the model section is part of the artifact signature, so all
stages have to run:

```bash
python src/01_data_generation/generate_operational_data.py   # then set data.generated_dates
python src/03_transforming_input_data/transform.py
python src/04_patch_indexing/index_patches.py
python src/05_model_training/train.py
python src/06_holdout_test/holdout_test.py
```

Small end-to-end check of the same code path (about 20 minutes). It needs a
small generated corpus under `data/99_smoke/01_generated/<date>` and that date in
`configs/smoke_v2.json`; the corpus used on 2026-11-15 has been deleted:

```bash
bash scripts/smoke_v2.sh          # inside WSL; uses configs/smoke_v2.json
```

The holdout now scores 7 forecast tokens with equal weights
(`scoring_protocol_version = complete_block_weighted_v3`). Its numbers are not
comparable with the four-token 80/16/3/1 results of the 500M model; the per-token
table in `test_metrics.json` allows a like-for-like comparison of patches 1–4.
Its leaderboard is ordered by `table_mase_median` and lists `table_mase_p10`,
`table_mase_p90` and `table_mase_profile` for every model. The pooled `mase`
is still written, for continuity only (section 9).

the unmodified repository). The 41 new tests are in

transform, index_patches, 10 minutes of training, resume from the checkpoint, holdout

`trimmed_table_loss` fell from 0.416 (untrained) to 0.251 after 1,378 updates

## 8. Pilot trainings

Five variants were each trained once for one pass over the same 25,341 generated
tables (3,338 updates of 8 tables, same seed, order and schedule; 6 to 9 minutes of
training each), then scored on 600 reserved validation tables and on a 2,611-request
holdout. The table is in `experiments/claude_review/pilot_summary_2026-11-15.json`
(written by `pilot_summary.py`). The pilot corpora, checkpoints and logs were
deleted afterwards; to repeat them, generate a small corpus and run
`scripts/smoke_v2.sh` with variants of `configs/smoke_v2.json`.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **A. Default (all changes)** | 221.5M | 8–9 min | 0.2399 | 5.80 | 5.02 | 3.21 |
| B. Without temporal attention | 203.8M | 7.9 min | 0.2410 | 5.83 | 5.12 | 3.23 |
| C. Constructor initialisation | 221.5M | 8.2 min | 0.2413 | 5.84 | 5.12 | 3.24 |
| D. Wider and shallower, 1024 × 13 | 215.4M | 6.2 min | 0.2407 | 5.76 | 5.15 | 3.21 |
| E. Without `asinh` | 221.5M | 8.6 min | not comparable | 5.81 | 5.18 | 3.36 |
| Naive (last value) | | | | | 6.06 | 3.73 |
| Chronos-2 | | | | | 4.57 | 2.45 |
| Timestral | | | | | 4.18 | 2.14 |

How to read this:

- **It is one run per variant after about 0.5% of one production epoch.** It shows
  how quickly each variant starts to learn, not where it ends. Differences of a
  percent or less on validation are too small to call from single runs.
- Every variant trained stably: no out-of-memory retries, and at most 0.2% of
  the last 1,000 updates clipped (1.2% without `asinh`). All overtook the naive
  forecast within minutes. None is close to the pretrained competitors yet, as
  expected at this budget.
- The default is the best or tied on every column but one, and each ablation is a
  little worse than it on the holdout (2–3% on median table MASE). That is
  consistent with temporal attention, the upstream initialisation and `asinh`
  each helping slightly. It is not proof.
- Variant E measures its validation loss in standardised rather than `asinh`
  units. Its 99th-percentile table loss is 7.7 against 0.54 for the default: without
  `asinh` a few tables dominate the loss again, as in the 500M run.
- Variant D saw the same data in 25–30% less time and ended slightly behind the
  default at equal data. At equal time it would have seen more. If training time
  turns out to be the limit, `width 1024, layers 13, variate_heads 16` is the
  alternative shape to try.

## 9. Missing values, flat histories and the pooled MASE

The current generator produces missing values (12% of tables have missing target
values; 1.5% have a stretch longer than the forecast block).

**Rule: a missing value is never scored and never a reason to drop a table.**

| Where | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Model input | A missing point is an input like any other: value 0 after standardisation plus a mask bit. Statistics use observed points only. |
| Training labels | Missing labels carry no loss. |
| Training, target not observed yet | A position whose target has no observation so far (a table that starts with an outage) is not supervised: it has no location or scale. This was 0.04% of labels. |
| Training, nothing observed | A microbatch or a whole update without any observed label is skipped. It used to stop the run with `Microbatch has no observed training labels`, and a resume would have hit the same batch again. |
| Validation | Every request is forecast. Missing labels are ignored; a request is unscored only if all its labels are missing or the target has no history. Counts: `unscored_forecasts_without_observed_labels`, `unscored_forecasts_without_observed_history`. It used to raise `Invalid validation forecast`. |
| Holdout | Every model forecasts every origin that has target history, and the forecasts are saved, including for missing points. Missing points have weight zero. An origin whose labels are all missing has no score (`unscored_origin_targets_without_observed_labels`). |
| Holdout, no history | An origin where the target has never been observed is skipped for all models (`skipped_origins_without_observed_history`): the naive forecast and the MASE scale do not exist there. It used to raise. |

**Is the loss dominated by flat-then-spike examples?** Without `asinh` yes, with
it no, and the weight updates were never dominated either way. Measured on 33.3M
supervised labels from 3,311 training tables with the pilot models
(`experiments/claude_review/training_loss_concentration.py`). "Size" is the
label's distance from the mean so far, in standard deviations so far; a spike
after a near-constant history has an enormous one.

| Label size | Share of labels | Share of loss, `asinh` (default) | Share of loss, no `asinh` | Share of gradient at the head |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| up to 3 | 94.05% | 83.5% | 0.000002% | 92.9% |
| 3 to 10 | 5.20% | 10.3% | 0.0000005% | 6.1% |
| 10 to 100 | 0.58% | 3.5% | 0.0000006% | 0.74% |
| 100 to 10,000 | 0.15% | 1.8% | 0.000004% | 0.20% |
| above 10,000 | 0.03% | 1.0% | 99.99999% | 0.04% |

- With `asinh`, the worst 1% of tables carry 2.2% of the loss. Without it they
  carry 99.99999%: the loss value is then a statement about 0.03% of the labels.
- The gradient column is the same with and without `asinh` (to within 0.01
  percentage points). Pinball loss has a
  bounded slope: however far a label lies from the forecast, each quantile
  receives a gradient of at most its quantile level. Extreme labels inflate the
  number that is logged, not the direction the weights move in.
- What `asinh` does change for training is the input side (standardised inputs
  up to 10,000 become at most 9.9) and the meaning of the logged loss. Without
  it 1.2% of updates were clipped, with it 0.2%.

Nothing further was changed for these examples. They are legitimate data: after
a flat history the right forecast is "stays flat, with a small chance of a large
move", and that is what the quantiles are asked to learn.

**Pooled MASE on the new data.** In the pilot holdout one request with a
numerically constant 32-point history (it varies by about 1e-12, so its MASE
scale is 5e-13 rather than exactly zero) contributes essentially 100% of the
pooled MASE of every model: about 6.7 billion for Basin, Chronos-2, Timestral
and the naive forecast alike. This is why validation and the holdout now lead
with the median and the 10th and 90th percentile of table MASE.

**A defect of this work, found and fixed before use.** The rotary position
tables of the temporal attention were first stored as buffers that are not saved
with the weights. `RWKVForecaster.load` builds an empty model and fills it from
the file, which left those tables uninitialised in every loaded model: training
and validation were correct, holdout and forecasting were not (pilot A's holdout
median table MASE was 5.29 with the defect and 5.02 without). The tables are now
built on first use, and a test reloads a saved model and requires identical
forecasts.

## 10. Limits and open points

- **Nothing here is validated at scale.** Each change is backed by a measured
  defect or mechanism in the 500M run, not by a measured gain of the new model.
- All changes are switched on together, as requested. If the result disappoints,
  every option can be turned off individually in `configs/config.json`, at the
  cost of a new transform and index_patches (the model section is part of the artifact
  signature even for options that do not affect the data).
- `asinh` changes what the loss rewards: errors on extreme standardised values
  count less in training. The headline pooled MASE is a mean dominated by a few
  extreme requests, so a better typical forecast can coincide with a worse pooled
  mean. Compare `table_mase_median` as well as `mase`.
- Selecting weights on table MASE ignores the quality of the quantile forecasts.
  If the intervals matter as much as the median, watch `trimmed_table_loss` too.
- Stateful recurrent rollout (`recurrent=True` in fast inference) is not available
  for the hybrid model; the default full-context forecast is.
- Training CUDA graphs (`training_cuda_graphs`, off by default) support only the
  archived layout.
- The portable exports in `src/08_model_export` implement the 500M architecture
  only and refuse a hybrid checkpoint with a clear message.
- Not implemented from the reviews: sign-flip augmentation (B4, excluded),
  Muon (not recommended there), a median-anchored quantile head, auxiliary
  covariate targets and batch-size experiments (forecasting review only).
